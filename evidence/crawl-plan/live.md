# Live 429 test: per-store or IP-wide? (2026-10-31 01:44-02:04 UTC, this container, prototype wt-backoff, publishing off)
**Setup.** The registry seed lists 1,070 Shopify stores on 1,045 distinct hosts. 200 hosts were picked by sha256("live429-2026-10-31:"+roaster_id); the first 100 became group 1 and the rest group 2 (live/live_groups.json). The lake was local only (live/lake, since deleted), HF_TOKEN was unset and HfApi was stubbed to raise. Robots.txt, the User-Agent and per-host spacing were left unchanged. 696 requests in total, under a hard 700 cap. Raw log: live/live_responses.jsonl, one line per response (ts, host, status, Retry-After, nominal and effective rate). Limiter-parked attempts are flagged and excluded below.

**Concentration.** All 67 429s fell on 13 hosts. Each of those hosts got a 429 on its first /products.json, seconds after its own robots.txt returned 200, and kept getting 429s: the next request to the same host was another 429 in 45 of 53 cases. Meanwhile the other 87-95 hosts got 200s. Every Retry-After was 60. The hosts were mostly large, well-known roasters (Counter Culture, Sightglass, Temple, Equator, Zoka, Bongo Java and others).
**Timing.** The main-pass 429s are spread out: one new host every 10-60 s. A 429 does not make another host's next request more likely to get one (B: 24/296 after another host's 429 in the previous 30 s, against 6/22 otherwise). The only bursts are the deferred rounds, where all parked stores wake after the same 60 s and get 429s together. In A the share of 429s with another host's 429 within 10 s is 0.81, against 0.56 shuffled (p=0.05). In B it is 0.90 against 0.94 (p=0.94), so B shows no clustering.
**Rate.** Going from 1 to 3 req/s raised the stores hit from 5 to 8 per 100, with overlapping intervals. Requests per 429 store stayed flat, at about 3-4 retries each. Phase C repeated at about 0.1 req/s and the same 5 stores still answered 429 on their first request. Neither 1 nor 3 req/s fixes them.
**Runner comparison.** 71 of our 200 stores were in the 10-30 runner set. Of the 24 that got 429s on the runner, 22 got none here. Counter Culture, Cat & Cloud and Bongo Java had no 429 on the runner but answered 429 every time here. So the two IPs saw different stores fail (8 of our 13 have no runner row).
**Prototype bug found.** The burst detector treats a deferred round's synchronized retries as a platform-wide block. That happened 6 times: "5 of 5 stores blocked within 30 s: platform-wide". Each one paused Shopify for 60 s and halved the rate (down to 0.25 req/s in C). Deferred rounds should be excluded from burst detection, or their retries staggered.
**Verdict.**
- From this IP: per-store. A fixed 5-8% of stores refuse /products.json from this IP whatever the rate. Everything else stays at 200 at 3 req/s, with no 430s and no cross-host contagion.
- For the GitHub runners: inconclusive. Different IP, different stores failing, 29% of stores hit. This test can't rule out an IP-wide bucket on runner IPs.
- Would the prototype alone have finished the weekly from an IP like this one? Yes. The main pass ran at about 3 req/s (95 s per 100 stores, so ~17 min for ~1,050). Deferral costs about 2 x 60 s rounds plus the false pauses above. About 5-8% of Shopify stores would stay failing, so the ok rate would be about 92-94%, against 98.8% on the runner. 2 of 5 recovered after ~20 min.
